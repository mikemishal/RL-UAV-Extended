# Manuscript notes — route-level intent selection as the A1 tactical interface (rejected as specified)

## Motivation
Five heading-level interfaces failed (continuous steering, bounded residual, filtered residual, obstacle-primitive reuse, finite pulses) and a sixth (gated deterministic evasion blend) was rejected on safety and mission grounds: a small heading perturbation hands the frozen reactive obstacle layer states it cannot recover. We therefore moved above low-level guidance: A1 selects a *route intent* and the existing deterministic, perception-constrained planner generates and follows the path, with the frozen obstacle layer retaining final authority.

## Hypothesis (pre-registered)
An intelligent attacker can evade a defender safely by selecting a route-level tactical intent while deterministic obstacle-aware planning generates and follows the path. Accepted only if G1 (zero gained collisions at every clutter level, for every scripted request policy), G2 (no mission pathology), G3 (meaningful, positive authority), G4 (path efficiency), G5 (perception contract) and G6 (planner stability) hold for at least one (R, T_replan) configuration.

## Protocol
Intents DIRECT / LEFT / RIGHT / ABOVE; subgoals from the closest point of the attacker→asset segment to the defender offset by R ∈ {5, 7.5, 10} m (lateral, lateral, ceiling-capped vertical); planning attacker → subgoal → asset with the existing visibility-graph A* over the perceived static set; cadence T_replan ∈ {1, 2} s; validation before commitment, completion back to DIRECT, defender-information gate, and a post-completion hold of one replan interval (added after a four-scenario smoke test and before any sweep). Observation 43-D, reward v2. Deterministic sweep, no PPO, matched seeds 145000–145099, eight scripted request policies including random and adversarial switching, DIRECT (bit-identical to canonical A0) as the matched reference. Executed staged because of cost: C0 × four defenders and C1–C3 × {greedy, PN} (48,000 episodes); RMPC and the other C1–C3 defenders were not run (G1/G2 are monotone against a baseline with zero collisions and zero exhaustions).

## Quantitative findings (per configuration, 1,000 matched episodes per policy; DIRECT: 97 captures, 0 collisions, 0 exhaustions, max path-excess 1.06×)
- Safety: gained collisions 673–990 of 7,000 tactical episodes (none at C0; worst policy 12–25 / 35–67 / 88–99 per 200 at C1 / C2 / C3). 237 of 240 re-simulated collisions occurred while the frozen layer already owned steering, 179 more than three steps after the route had ended.
- Mission: endurance exhaustion 1,985–3,306 (vs 0); fly-by episodes 58–80% (vs 0%); orbit ≥ 1 revolution 1–28; >5× path excess 1,031–1,686 (88–91% exhaustions).
- Authority: up to 351 interception→capture conversions against 68 reversals (net +283 captures, ABOVE at R = 5 m, T = 2 s); the max-separation policy and one alternating configuration are net-negative.
- Perception: ≈ 160,000 planner calls, 0 without defender information, 0 with obstacle ids outside the perceived set; controller module free of world-state references; planner is static-only.
- Stability: no non-finite values, deterministic replay, route runs ≤ 22 steps, ≈ 2 waypoints per route, 30–37% of planner attempts rejected at validation (mostly "defender not ahead"); the pre-registered rapid-replanning bound (≤ 3 calls in 4 steps) was exceeded (6) because rejected validations of an every-step request count as calls.

## Interpretation
Route-level planning does what heading-level control could not: the evasion is effective (captures rise several-fold in scripted policies) and collisions no longer occur during the maneuver. The failure moved downstream. With the subgoal near the mission line close to a defender that guards the asset, a survived evasion leaves the attacker flying past the asset; the fast point-mass vehicle then needs a long turn, the episode lasts about five times longer than the DIRECT baseline (which ends in ≈ 10 steps by capture or interception), and the extra exposure produces both endurance exhaustion and collisions in clutter. The scripted always-on policies exaggerate this, but even the gentlest policy (max-separation) gains collisions. The result supports a more specific hypothesis than the one tested: the interface needs a mission-aware commitment (when to stop evading and fly to the asset) and a bound on episode prolongation, not a different geometry for the evasion itself.

## Limitations
Scripted request policies only (a learned selector was not trained, by design); RMPC and the remaining C1–C3 defenders not run; per-level pooling is 400 (C0) or 200 (C1–C3) episodes per policy; fixed defenders; G6's rapid-replanning metric counts cheap validation rejections; the pre-registered G2 compares exhaustion with a baseline that cannot exhaust, so it also counts survived-but-unconverted evasions; the static map is fully known in the canonical perception contract, so the audit demonstrates, rather than stresses, the perception restriction (a `local`-awareness counterfactual test does).

## Decision
**Architecture rejected as specified** (verdict C); G3 authority is a positive finding. Retained: reward v2, the strict matched-gate methodology, DIRECT ≡ A0 wrapper equivalence, and the perception-audit machinery. Candidate next step (to be pre-registered as a new action version, not applied here): mission-aware route commitment with bounded episode prolongation.
