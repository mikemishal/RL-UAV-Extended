## Cross-cutting findings
1. Designs 2, 3, 5 and 6 fail on the same boundary (a heading perturbation hands the reactive layer states it cannot recover); design 7 moves above that boundary and the failure mode changes: collisions no longer occur on the maneuver but after a *survived* encounter.
2. Authority and benefit are separable: design 7 is the interface whose scripted policies raise the capture count most (e.g. +283 / 1,000 for ABOVE at R = 5 m, T = 2 s), which is evidence that route-level choice carries real tactical value.
3. The pre-registered gates (zero gained collisions, no endurance exhaustion relative to a DIRECT baseline that ends in ≈ 10 steps) penalize any interface that prolongs episodes; any future interface must bound episode prolongation or commit to the asset after evasion by construction.
