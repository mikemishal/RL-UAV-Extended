# Learning-Benefit Diagnostic Study

Status: exploratory / pilot analysis. **This study does NOT use the final
journal evaluation seed bank.** Results here are diagnostic evidence used to
decide the NEXT controller architecture; they are not themselves journal
performance claims.

Environment frozen at tag `clutter-env-v1` (commit `93474b98a6a9ff909b0bc808b2117afb2d601958`).
Diagnostic framework committed at `b8d9234883e4b9da54d42887ecce06ccd8eeeb1e`
on branch `analysis/learning-benefit`.

## 1. Purpose

Answer: *when* does learning (LR-PPO) outperform strong analytical guidance
(CV Lead), *why*, and *what specific analytical-model deficiency* is being
corrected — as evidence for designing the next controller, not as a
replacement controller itself.

## 2. Controllers compared

- **Lead** — deployable constant-velocity (CV) pure-pursuit intercept
  controller (`uav_defend/policies/baseline/lead_intercept_policy.py`),
  refactored onto a pure helper (`lead_math.py`) and regression-verified
  byte-identical to the pre-refactor implementation.
- **CA-Lead** — analysis-only constant-acceleration variant using a quartic
  intercept-time solver and finite-difference acceleration estimate
  (`uav_defend/policies/analysis/constant_acceleration_lead_policy.py`).
  Falls back to CV Lead when the acceleration estimate is ~0 or no valid
  positive real root exists.
- **True-State Lead** — **analysis-only oracle, NOT a deployable baseline.**
  Uses the exact same CV intercept math as Lead but is fed ground-truth
  hostile position/velocity directly, via a deliberately non-standard method
  signature (`act(defender_position, true_hostile_position,
  true_hostile_velocity, ...)`, not `act(obs, info)`) so it can never be
  substituted for a deployable policy. It isolates how much of Lead's
  shortfall is attributable to sensing/estimation error versus other causes.
- **LR-PPO** — pre-trained residual-PPO-over-Lead checkpoint (seed 55,
  250k steps), loaded by absolute path from an external checkpoint store; not
  retrained or modified in this study.

## 3. Scenario battery (6 scenarios, seeds 60000–60199, N=200/controller/scenario)

All parameter values reused from existing robustness protocols (never
invented): `nominal_open` (defaults), `strong_maneuver`
(`defender_max_accel=4.0`, `defender_max_turn_rate_deg=45.0`),
`strong_evasion` (`enemy_evasion_gain=1.0`), `mobility_mismatch`
(`v_d=12.0`, `v_e=16.0`), `clutter` (obstacles enabled, random layout,
8 obstacles), `clutter_strong_maneuver` (clutter + restrictive dynamics
combined).

A smaller N=40 seed sub-sample (same seed range) was used for full per-step
diagnostic logging (`experiments/learning_benefit/diagnostic_logging.py`),
capturing Lead/CA intercept solutions, LR-PPO residual angle, true/estimated
hostile state, defender dynamics/saturation flags, and geometry at every
step.

## 6. CV prediction-error findings (retrospective interpolation, Lead controller, N=40 episodes)

Using `experiments/learning_benefit/prediction_error.py`'s interpolation-based
retrospective computation (no causal claim — descriptive only), the CV
intercept point's error against the actual realized future hostile position:

| scenario | mean error | median | p90 | p95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nominal_open | 52.3 | 38.5 | 117.3 | 143.4 |
| strong_maneuver | 52.0 | 32.8 | 122.1 | 148.4 |
| strong_evasion | 51.5 | 37.5 | 115.5 | 143.8 |
| **mobility_mismatch** | **326.5** | **160.0** | **730.3** | **1221.9** |
| clutter | 30.1 | 14.5 | 79.7 | 110.4 |
| clutter_strong_maneuver | 46.2 | 26.5 | 112.3 | 149.0 |

`mobility_mismatch` shows a 6–10x larger CV prediction error than every other
scenario, consistent with much longer intercept horizons (slow defender vs.
fast hostile) amplifying sensing/velocity-estimate error under linear
extrapolation.

## 7. LR-PPO residual-angle correlations (episode-level aggregation, anti-pseudoreplication)

| signal | Pearson r | n |
|---|---|---|
| hostile turn rate | **0.53** | 240 |
| defender acceleration-saturation fraction | 0.43 | 240 |
| defender turn-saturation fraction | 0.42 | 240 |
| evasion-active fraction | 0.12 | 240 |
| obstacle-avoidance-active fraction | −0.16 | 240 |
| episode-mean Lead CV prediction error | −0.07 (pooled); range −0.02 to 0.35 per scenario | 225 |

Residual magnitude correlates most strongly with hostile maneuvering and
defender dynamic saturation, not with raw CV prediction-error magnitude.
Correlational only — no causal claim.

## 8. Negative result: clutter + strong maneuver

LR-PPO (trained without obstacles present) shows a statistically resolved
**negative** transfer in `clutter_strong_maneuver` (−11.5pp vs. Lead, CI
excludes zero), while True-State Lead still shows a modest, non-significant
positive gain there. This indicates an out-of-distribution generalization
failure in the existing LR-PPO checkpoint, not evidence against learning in
general.

## 9. Constant-Acceleration Lead

CA-Lead does not consistently improve on Lead in any scenario and is
significantly *worse* in `mobility_mismatch` (−9.0pp) — a naive
finite-difference acceleration estimate amplifies noise over long horizons
rather than correcting for it.

## 10. Interpretation of dominant mechanisms

No single mechanism explains all scenarios (mixed evidence):

1. **Sensing/estimation error** dominates in `mobility_mismatch` (True-State
   ≈ LR-PPO, both closing ~18pp of the gap to Lead; CV error 6–10x baseline).
2. **Dynamic-feasibility limits**, not sensing, dominate in
   `strong_maneuver` (True-State gives no benefit, yet LR-PPO still gains;
   residual angle correlates strongly with saturation fractions and hostile
   turn rate).
3. **Obstacle-interaction / OOD generalization failure** dominates in
   `clutter_strong_maneuver` (LR-PPO regresses below Lead).
4. Easy/moderate scenarios (`nominal_open`, `strong_evasion`, `clutter`
   alone) cluster near a high ceiling with modest gaps for all controllers.
